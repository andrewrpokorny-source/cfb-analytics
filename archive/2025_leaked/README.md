# Archived 2025 outputs — do not trust these numbers

Moved here 2026-08-04. Both files were produced by models trained with a
**target leak**, so their headline results are not achievable.

## What happened

`honest_backfill.py` split train/test by date (`SPLIT_DATE = "2025-08-01"`),
which looks like a clean walk-forward. But it fed each 2025 game features from
`/ratings/srs?year=2025`, and that endpoint returns a single **full-season**
rating per team — computed from the results of the very games being predicted.
SRS carried 54% of the spread model's feature importance, so the outcome was
effectively an input.

## Measured impact

Controlled rerun, train on 2024 → test on 2025, n=1584:

| Arm | ATS | ML | Totals |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 SRS (what produced `backtest_2025.csv`) | **58.7%** | 78.7% | 52.4% |
| Prior-year SRS (clean) | **50.7%** | 75.4% | 52.9% |
| Always bet home | 50.9% | — | — |
| Always bet the favorite (SU) | — | 77.1% | — |

Break-even at -110 is 52.38%. The clean model has no edge on the spread, and
its moneyline picks are worse than blindly backing favorites.

The leak is visible directly in the correlation with the post-market residual:

- same-season `srs_diff` vs `ats_diff`: **+0.256**
- prior-season `srs_diff` vs `ats_diff`: **+0.003**

## Files

- `backtest_2025.csv` — 1580 graded 2025 games, reported 58.7% ATS. Leaked.
- `live_predictions.csv` — live picks through the Jan 2026 bowls, generated by
  `.pkl` models trained the same way. Also leaked.

## Replacement

The `wf/` package rebuilds this correctly. `wf/dataset.py` documents the
as-of-week contract, and `python -m wf.audit` fails if any feature starts
showing suspicious residual correlation. Use those numbers instead.
